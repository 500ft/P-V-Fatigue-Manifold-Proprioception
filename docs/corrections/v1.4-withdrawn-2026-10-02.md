# v1.4 withdrawn, 2026-10-02

The v1.4 candidate will not be released. On 2026-10-02 the owner chose to stop
the paper and keep the code, after a review found that its main positive claim
doesn't hold up once the comparison is made at matched cost. This note records
what was checked, what still stands, and what was not rerun.

## What was found

**At matched cost, a cycle clock ties the P-V trigger.** Study 3 chose the
clock's period on train actuators as the longest period within the error
budget. That rule picked 2,700 cycles, which recalibrates less often than the
trigger and misses the budget on the held-out actuators. A clock every 2,400 or
2,500 cycles gives exactly the trigger's recalibration schedule on all 20
actuators, so its error is identical too.

| Policy | Recalibrations per held-out actuator | Held-out error | Train error |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| P-V trigger, τ = 0.05 | 2.00 | 0.058 mm | 0.044 mm |
| Clock, every 2,400 cycles | 2.00 | 0.058 mm | 0.044 mm |
| Clock, every 2,700 cycles (the one v1.4 reports) | 1.50 | 0.208 mm | 0.132 mm |

The error budget is 0.159 mm. The v1.4 text calls 1.5 against 2 recalibrations
"a similar recalibration count". It isn't: the cheaper clock is the reason the
trigger looked better.

**The health signal is the compliance multiplier read back out.** In Study 3,
normalized loop area equals the normalized compliance multiplier that drives the
pose drift, to within 2.3 × 10⁻¹⁴. It is also the same curve for all 20
actuators (largest spread 3.3 × 10⁻¹⁴), computed with rest time set to zero and
no probe noise. That is why the trigger fires at the same life stage on every
actuator, and why a fixed clock can match it. So r = 0.885 measures how
linearly one function of the simulated fatigue state follows another, not
whether a P-V probe can detect fatigue. It falls below 1 partly because the
relationship is nonlinear; estimator error on the pose side also contributes.

## What still stands

- The Study 3 numbers are correct simulation outputs and are kept unchanged:
  2 recalibrations per actuator against 5 for always-on, 0.058 mm held-out
  error, the non-positive lead at τ = 0.05, and the clock comparison as it was
  originally selected. What fails is reading them as evidence that P-V sensing
  adds value over counting cycles.
- The simulators, the dataset generator, the actuator-identity split, the
  preregistration records and the checks.
- The other findings as already stated: cross-talk through the shared supply is
  second order at the registered parameters, and Studies A to C limit the
  signal further.

## Reported but not rerun

The review also reported these. They are recorded here but were not
independently rerun:

- The model's own Mullins recovery after 24 hours and after a week of rest is a
  large share of the trigger threshold, and Study 3 sets rest to zero.
- With a fixed calibration, pose error is small from life 0.1 to 0.7 and jumps
  at 0.9, so that one grid point decides every policy comparison.
- The cross-talk sweep ages every chamber together with the focal chamber. The
  case the introduction motivates, one aged chamber among fresh ones, was never
  simulated.
- The P-V probe drives a sinusoidal volume, which needs a displacement pump, so
  "pressure-only" in the title doesn't fit the probe.
