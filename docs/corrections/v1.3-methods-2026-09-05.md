## Verified correction

Study 3 calls `health_trajectory` in [run_study3.py](../../scripts/run_study3.py).
That helper uses `fatigue_state(..., 0.0, ...)` and an analytic SLS loop-area
calculation in [coupling.py](../../pipeline/coupling.py). Thus its policy signal
is an idealized model-derived probe, **not the noisy, quantized, decimated volume
channel in the Phase D dataset**. Its rest input is zero; varying rest/recovery
is not a Study 3 robustness intervention. Study 1 and the sensor simulator do
not establish that these perturbations reach the Study 3 health signal.

Both idealized loop area and pose drift share the fatigue generator. The
reported correlation is therefore structurally favored; it is not diagnostic
robustness to probe noise, rest or alternative degradation mechanisms. No result
values or historical plots are regenerated in this correction.

`policy_metrics` averages stage-level pose RMSE across the five sampled life
stages, then results aggregate actuator-level values. Meeting the 0.159 mm budget
means meeting that **macro-average**, not a maximum-error, all-time, all-actuator
or safety guarantee. The budget is a train-derived modeling choice, not a task
tolerance supplied by an application. The two-versus-five count includes initial
calibration; 60% fewer counted calibration events is not a measured downtime or
total-cost saving.
