Soft pneumatic grippers face two coupled deployment problems: silicone chambers fail by cyclic fatigue, and pressure-only proprioception relies on the same in-loop signals. We study, in simulation, whether the in-loop pressure-volume (P-V) hysteresis loop can serve as a health indicator of fatigue-driven proprioception degradation in the practical shared-manifold topology, where chambers share one supply. From 2,000 labeled traces (20 actuators x 5 life stages x 2 topologies x 2 contact states x 5 reps), split by actuator identity, we evaluate static and dynamic pressure-only correctors and four recalibration policies. Four findings, two negative. (1) The predicted shared-manifold advantage of a dynamic (lagged-input) corrector does not materialize: cross-talk is real but second-order, and the dynamic corrector improves pose error by about 0%. (2) The dominant error is the topology-independent fatigue compliance drift, which grows a young-calibrated estimator's curvature error by roughly two orders of magnitude over life. (3) The observable P-V loop area is a strong health indicator of that drift (Pearson r = 0.885, 95% CI [0.835, 0.958] on held-out actuators), but a lead-time audit found no positive temporal lead at the deployed threshold (median lead -0.123 normalized life, 6/6 actuators nonpositive); a descriptive frontier shows lead is purchasable: tau = 0.01 gives positive lead for all held-out actuators (median +0.346) while still using 40% fewer recalibrations than always-on. (4) P-V-triggered recalibration meets a stated accuracy budget at 60% fewer recalibrations than always-on (2 vs 5 per actuator); a sensing-free cycle-count schedule tuned by the same rule fails the budget on held-out actuators. Caveats: all actuators come from one generative model, and absolute pose errors are sub-millimeter, so operational value grows with tighter accuracy requirements.
